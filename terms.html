2. Diners

  • Wasabi is free for diners. You don't need an account to order; a first name is enough.
  • You order from the restaurant, not from Wasabi. The restaurant decides its menu, prices, what's available and how it serves you, and is responsible for your food and drinks, including allergy information. Ask the staff if you have an allergy.
  • Your bill is with the restaurant. Wasabi shows what was ordered and works out how a table's bill can be split. It is a helpful calculation, not a tax receipt. You pay the restaurant directly. Complaints, refunds and questions about an order or a bill are between you and the restaurant.
  • Order for your own table. Only order on a table you are sitting at, with the people you're with. Friends who join with a table code need a yes from someone already at the table.
  • Accounts. You must be at least [18] to create an account. Children can order at a table with their family as a guest, with no account. If you sign in with a phone number or Google, keep that phone or account secure: anyone who can use it can use your Wasabi account. You can delete your account and visits at any time from your account page.
  • Beware of scams. Wasabi will never phone, text or WhatsApp you to ask for your sign-in code, a mobile money PIN or a card number. Only type a sign-in code into wasabi.co.tz yourself, and never share it.
  • Offers are optional. A restaurant can only send you offers if you ticked the box for that restaurant, and you can stop them at any time (see section 6).

3. Restaurants

  • Joining. A restaurant registers on the sign-up page. We check the details and switch the restaurant on. We can decline a registration.
  • Who uses your account. The owner signs in with the Google account registered for the restaurant and can add managers. Waiters use a PIN, and kitchen, bar and grill phones use staff codes. You are responsible for everyone you give access to, for keeping PINs and staff codes private, and for changing them when someone leaves (Tables & QR → Make new staff codes; Staff → change a PIN).
  • Your menu and prices. You are responsible for your menu: names, descriptions, prices, what is sold out, and allergy and other information diners need. Prices diners see are the prices you set in Wasabi.
  • Your business, licences and taxes. You remain responsible for running your business: licences, food safety, alcohol rules, service, payments, and taxes, including issuing fiscal (EFD) receipts as the law requires. Wasabi's bills and statements don't replace them.
  • Table QR codes. Each table's QR code carries a secret key. Keep printed table cards on the tables, and print new ones if a card goes missing or is copied.
  • A backup plan. Wasabi needs the internet and working phones. Keep a simple way to take orders without it (for example, a paper pad) in case of a power, network or service outage.

4. Fees, renewals and cancelling

  • Price. TSh 130,000 a month or TSh 1,300,000 a year, plus any taxes that apply, unless we agree a different price with you in writing. Any free trial is agreed in writing and ends on the date shown on your owner page.
  • Paying in advance. Fees are paid in advance for each month or year. Your owner page shows the date you're paid until.
  • Late payment. If a payment is late, ordering keeps working for a grace period of 7 days after the paid-until date. After that, diners and staff can't order until you pay. Your menu, settings and history are kept and come back when you renew.
  • Price changes. We'll tell you at least 30 days before a price change, and it applies from your next renewal.
  • Cancelling. You can cancel at any time by telling us (see Contact us). Service continues until the end of the period you've paid for; we don't refund part-used periods, except where the law requires it or section 10 says so.
  • Your data after you leave. For 90 days after your service ends, we keep your restaurant's data so you can come back, and we'll give you a copy of your menu and order history on request. After 90 days we delete it, except what we must keep by law (for example, our own accounting records).

11. Data processing (for restaurants)

This section is the data processing agreement between each restaurant and Wasabi under Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act, 2022 and its regulations.

Who is responsible for what

  • The restaurant is the controller of the personal data of its diners and staff that is used to run its service through Wasabi: first names, orders, table activity, calls and bills, waiters' names, and offer choices for that restaurant.
  • Wasabi is the processor of that data and handles it only to provide Wasabi to the restaurant, as described in these terms and the way the restaurant uses the app, or where the law requires.
  • Wasabi is the controller of diner accounts (sign-in details and visits across restaurants), owner and manager sign-in details, and its own business records, as described in the Privacy policy.

Wasabi's commitments as processor

  • Purpose. We use the restaurant's data only to run Wasabi for that restaurant, never for our own marketing, and we never sell it.
  • Confidentiality. Only people who need to, to run and support Wasabi, can access it, and they must keep it confidential.
  • Security. We protect it with measures including encrypted connections, database rules tested automatically before every release that limit who can read or change each record, restaurant data kept apart from other restaurants, secret table keys, staff PINs and codes, and restricted administrator access.
  • Sub-processors. We use Google LLC (Firebase: database, sign-in and notifications; data stored in Johannesburg, South Africa), Cloudflare, Inc. (the service that rings waiters' phones) and GitHub, Inc. (hosting the website's code). We'll tell restaurants before adding or replacing a sub-processor, and they can object.
  • Data outside Tanzania. Because these providers store or handle data outside Tanzania, we rely on their data protection terms and on the approvals required under Tanzanian law.
  • Helping you. We'll help the restaurant answer diners' requests to see, correct or delete their data, and with any questions from the Personal Data Protection Commission.
  • Breaches. If we become aware of a breach affecting the restaurant's data, we'll tell the restaurant without undue delay and within 48 hours, with what we know and what we're doing about it.
  • Keeping and deleting. Guests' first names on orders, calls and tables are replaced with "Guest" after 30 days. Order records are kept for 12 months, then deleted, keeping only totals without names. When a restaurant leaves, section 4 applies.
  • Information. On request, we'll give the restaurant the information it reasonably needs to show it complies with the law.

The restaurant's commitments as controller

  • Use diners' and staff data lawfully, only for its service and for offers diners agreed to (section 6).
  • Keep staff access (PINs, codes, manager accounts) under control (section 3).
  • Tell us promptly if it receives a request or complaint about data in Wasabi.
